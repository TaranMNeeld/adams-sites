The Continuous Flow Model

Six agents do the work. One human applies the taste.

From raw idea to tested concept in 72 hours, with the evidence and the lineage to prove every step.

The one rule

Task to the agent. Judgment to the human.

If it can be specified as a procedure, the agent owns it and runs it without asking. If it requires a person to decide whether something is good, right, or worth continuing, the human owns it, and the agent surfaces it in a form they can judge at a glance.

The tools disappear. What remains is pure human judgment, finally free to do the only work it was ever meant to do.

The cycle

Six phases, three days.

Three agents run once on entry and produce the concept and the questions it must answer. Three more deliver it, Prototype and Validation loop, and Handoff closes it.

01

Intake & Frame

Turns the raw request into a framed problem, target user, constraints, and stakes. What it assumes becomes the first open questions.

02

Discovery

Grounds the framing in evidence, research, market scans, analogs, before anything is built. Retires some questions, opens others.

03

Concepts

Turns intent into buildable, testable claims. Each hypothesis is written so a study can score it.

04

Prototype

Builds a real, accessible artifact, concentrating fidelity where the open questions live, and deploys it to a permanent, versioned URL.

Loops with Validation · you judge every cycle

05

Validation

Picks the evidence source, writes and runs the study, and returns verdicts, themes, quotes, and a recommendation against the load-bearing question.

Loops with Prototype · you judge every cycle

06

Handoff

Does no judging. Once you ship, it assembles the immutable Concept Passport from the trail the loop already wrote.

The judgment contract

Your entire interface is seven words.

For each one, the agent does all the mechanical follow-through. You express intent; the agent realizes it. Three are reserved absolutely, they carry consequences a machine should not own.

Continue
The direction is right; press on. The agent drafts the next change, picks the source, and runs the next round.
Change this
Fix what I’m pointing at. The agent translates the note into prototype changes and re-tests.
Confirm it
I believe it, prove it on real people. Re-tests the same version on a higher-ceiling source. No rebuild.
Redirect
The concept is wrong, not the build. Routes back to the upstream agent that owns the faulty decision.
Ship
Good enough, and the evidence earns it. Freezes the trail and advances to Handoff.
Kill
Stop spending on this. Closes the concept with the reason recorded.
Accept the risk
A known gap is acceptable to carry. Records the item and its rationale in the Passport.

★ Reserved for the human. The agent may recommend Ship, Kill, or Accept the Risk, it advances, terminates, or proceeds on none of its own accord.

The deliverable

The Concept Passport.

Not a deck. A complete handoff package the receiving team can build from, starting from proven ground, not open questions.

  • Tested concepts, prioritized.
  • All evidence and study findings.
  • A go-forward call: build, iterate, or kill.
  • Every prototype at a permanent, openable URL.
  • A transition brief for the engineering team.
  • The round-by-round lineage, including any risk knowingly carried.
A client opening the Passport months later sees the exact artifact tested, the exact evidence, and the path that produced it.

Why it holds

Invariants, not trade-offs.

Immutable prototypes

The version lives in the URL; content never changes. Every version a concept passed through stays openable forever, beside its evidence.

A mandatory lint gate

No prototype reaches a study with broken requests, console errors, an unreachable task, or an accessibility failure. Failing builds regenerate automatically.

An evidence floor

Cheap sources can build working confidence, but never make a concept ship-eligible. Crossing the bar always takes a high-ceiling pass against the real question.
